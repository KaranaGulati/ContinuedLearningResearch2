# Novelty check: five psychology hypotheses on Polymarket data

Checked 20 October 2026 with about 11 targeted web searches plus the earlier literature review notes. A web search cannot prove that nothing exists, so run a final Google Scholar check before writing.

| Hypothesis | Done on Polymarket? | Closest existing work | What would be new |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Round-number thresholds draw more trading (round numbers as goals) | Not found | Pope & Simonsohn (2011) on round numbers as goals; a commercial blog (Pillarlab) claims round thresholds attract herd betting, with no data | First test of round-number salience in prediction-market thresholds |
| 2. Dread: frightening events are overpriced (availability heuristic) | Not found | Longshot bias by category (Cardozo & Rivero-Wildemauwe 2026, arXiv 2609.12878: Weather about −25%, Culture about −26%); "Yes bias" in mention markets (Deleep 2026, SSRN 6322678); Polymarket pulled its nuclear-detonation markets in March 2026 after they implied up to 19% | Coding questions by emotional content and comparing dread vs neutral questions at the same price, which separates dread from the general longshot and Yes biases |
| 3. Trades at round prices (50¢, 25¢) do worse | Not found for prediction markets | Kuo, Lin & Zhao (2015, Review of Financial Studies) on round-number limit orders in stocks; Kalshi and Polymarket trade in whole cents (1¢ ticks) | First test in prediction markets. Clustering can only be measured on the last digit of the cent price (0 and 5 vs others) |
| 4. Gambler's fallacy in repeating Up/Down markets | Not found | Casino and lottery studies (for example Croson & Sundali 2005; Clotfelter & Cook 1993); Dai, Jia & Yu (2026, arXiv 2606.31675) on settlement manipulation in 5-minute Bitcoin markets, not streaks | First test of streak pricing in repeated real-money binary markets |
| 5. Wishful thinking: hoped-for outcomes are overpriced | Partly done in other markets | Forsythe, Rietz & Ross (1999) on partisan wishful thinking in the Iowa Electronic Markets; Rothschild & Sethi (2015); no Polymarket test, and Zitzewitz's Polymarket extension is pending | Coding questions as good news vs bad news, not by trader politics |

Ranking by novelty: 1 and 4 are untouched, 2 and 3 are new tests of known effects, and 5 is the least new because wishful thinking in prediction markets dates back to 1999.
