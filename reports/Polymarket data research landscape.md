# Polymarket research crowds calibration, neglects the edges

As of October 2026, Polymarket data has been studied heavily on five fronts. These are aggregate calibration and the favorite-longshot bias, the 2024 US presidential election, trader profitability concentration, on-chain arbitrage, and LLM forecasting benchmarks. Several large-sample preprints on each front already exploit the full on-chain ledger of more than a billion trades. The open ground is elsewhere. Nobody has evaluated the 2026 platform changes as natural experiments: the staggered January–March 2026 taker-fee rollout, the April 2026 CLOB V2/pUSD migration, the reported August 2026 TWAP settlement switch, and the April 2026 insider indictments. Nobody has used Polymarket's macro, tariff and geopolitical odds as identification shocks. UMA oracle disputes have no quantitative study. No pre-registered benchmark exists for the 3 November 2026 midterms. Sports prices have not been compared with sharp closing lines, and LLM forecasts have not been scored against Polymarket prices and superforecasters on the same questions at the same moments. The most important context is that almost none of this literature is peer-reviewed. It is a 2025–2026 wave of arXiv, SSRN and NBER working papers, many of whose SSRN full texts could not be read for this review. Results are therefore provisional and often depend on the method. Favorite-longshot findings flip sign with the weighting scheme, wash-trading estimates span 1% to 25%, and heuristic trade-sign classifiers are close to random on Polymarket. The data stack also shifted in 2026: CLOB V2 went live on 28 April, the legacy SDKs were archived, Goldsky's public subgraphs went stale, and the Data API v1 retires on 24 October 2026. Any project that starts now needs a V2-aware pipeline. It also needs to start recording high-frequency data immediately, because sub-3-hour price history is not kept for long.

## Aggregate accuracy holds, but it fractures under conditioning

The headline numbers flatter Polymarket because of its base rate. The company's accuracy page reports that the leading outcome was correct **90.4% of the time one month out and 98.7% four hours out**, with an overall **Brier score of 0.059**. The same page shows that **78.1% of markets resolve "No"** and does not disclose the sample size, the date range or the horizon behind the Brier score ([Polymarket](https://polymarket.com/accuracy)). An informal analysis of 7,661 resolved binary markets finds Brier improving from **0.042 at 30 days to 0.025 at 24 hours**. The 0–5% bucket holds roughly three-quarters of that sample and resolved YES only 0.1% of the time ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/Hruc6Gwo3vBZFGb6v/empirical-calibration-of-polymarket-analysis-of-7-661)). Leading-outcome hit rates of 90–99% therefore mostly reflect a long tail of near-certain markets. The informative studies condition on category, price bucket and horizon.

Several of those conditioning studies have now appeared, and they agree that calibration depends on context. Reichenbach and Walther study **1.5 billion trades across 779,000 markets**. They find that prices are informative and become more so as markets mature. Weather markets are most precise and mention and geopolitics markets least precise, and in sports Polymarket matches professional bookmakers ([SSRN 7552098](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7552098); full text not accessed). Nam Anh Le decomposes calibration slopes across **353 million Kalshi and Polymarket trades**. The most robust result is **persistent underconfidence in political markets**: prices are compressed toward 50%, and this replicates on Polymarket. Le also cautions that roughly half of raw slope variation is estimation noise ([arXiv 2602.19520](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.19520)). Qin and Yang's Polymarket-v1 database paper uses ground-truth aggressor flags and finds that **true VPIN predicts worse Brier scores, while wider Gibbs spreads predict better ones**. They read this as niche, high-spread markets attracting informed specialists ([arXiv 2606.04217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04217)). Liquidity and accuracy are therefore not monotonically related, a point Clinton and Huang reinforce: trade count is unrelated to accuracy once exchange and contract type are controlled for ([SocArXiv PDF](https://osf.io/download/692db7dd37baa6ac72b550fb/)).

The favorite-longshot bias (FLB) is the most contested behavioral question, and the disagreement is mostly about measurement. Cardozo and Rivero-Wildemauwe study **588 million trades by 2.48 million accounts**. Purchases below 10¢ lose **19.3¢ per dollar** and purchases at or above 90¢ earn 0.83¢. Longshots lose 6.3¢ when contracts are equally weighted but *gain* 4.1¢ when grouped by parent event. The pattern is robust in crypto and politics and **absent in sports** ([arXiv 2609.12878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12878)). Reichenbach and Walther report no general longshot bias, but find that low-probability outcomes start underpriced and that "default" outcomes stay overpriced ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7552098)). Deleep et al. argue the apparent FLB is an artifact of a **"Yes bias"**, in which traders overpay for affirmative outcomes, especially in mention markets ([Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/how-wise-is-the-crowd-in-prediction-markets/)). In crypto threshold markets, extreme Bitcoin moves are persistently overpriced relative to Deribit option-implied densities ([Fabi et al., FC'26](https://fc26.ifca.ai/defi/papers/market-efficiency-prediction-markets.pdf)). The most coherent reading is threefold. Trade-weighted returns on cheap contracts are deeply negative. Event-grouped or lifecycle-controlled analyses shrink or reverse the effect. "Yes bias" and "default overpricing" are probably the same phenomenon seen from two angles. Any new FLB paper has to state its weighting scheme up front or it will add noise to this debate.

Comparisons with other forecasters depend on the unit of analysis. On the single headline 2024 contract, Polymarket beat polls, especially in swing states ([Cutting et al., arXiv 2507.08921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.08921)), and statistical models failed to beat the market on the presidency ([Sethi et al., ACM CI '25](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/162638)). Across broad cross-sections of political contracts the picture reverses. Clinton and Huang score **1,237 markets active on 4 November 2024** and find that **93% of PredictIt, 78% of Kalshi and 67% of Polymarket markets beat a coin flip** ([IDEAS/SocArXiv](https://ideas.repec.org/p/osf/socarx/d5yx2_v1.html)). Sethi reports that models outperformed the market on competitive states and congressional races ([Substack](https://rajivsethi.substack.com/p/markets-models-and-mamdani)). In the 2022 midterms, a log-score comparison ranked FiveThirtyEight above Polymarket, with only Metaculus scoring higher ([Asterisk](https://asteriskmag.com/issues/05/prediction-markets-have-an-elections-problem-jeremiah-johnson)). Cross-platform dashboards find that **no platform wins on every topic**; Kalshi narrowly leads overall, and Kalshi and Polymarket both do well on sports ([Manifund/Calibration City](https://manifund.org/projects/calibration-city)). On FOMC decisions, a Gothenburg thesis finds Polymarket and fed funds futures statistically indistinguishable over 26 meetings ([GUPEA](https://gupea.ub.gu.se/items/996923e1-337f-4bd2-ba95-3a3144f3ce9c)). On partisanship, Zitzewitz finds no systematic left-right bias in political markets from 1880 to 2025. Polymarket is **not yet in his sample**, and he says a post-midterm revision will add it ([NBER w35846](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35846)).

The 2025 record is thinner than its reputation suggests. Polymarket priced Cuomo around 93% in late May 2025 before Mamdani won the NYC primary ([Next Event Horizon](https://nexteventhorizon.substack.com/p/nyc-mayor-kalshi-polymarket)). About 75% of money backed Simion before Romania's runoff, which Nicușor Dan won ([Puterea.ro](https://www.puterea.ro/?p=485469)). Prevost sat near 1–2% before becoming pope ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/318672/american-cardinal-prevost-elected-pope-betting-markets-upset)). Each miss on its own is consistent with calibrated pricing. The point is that no one has scored the 2025 cycle in aggregate (Germany, Canada, Australia, Poland, Japan, Chile), so Polymarket's accuracy reputation still rests mainly on one presidential call. Short-horizon overreaction is documented only episode by episode. **58.3% of 182 national-presidential contracts showed significantly negative daily autocorrelation** in 2024 ([Clinton & Huang](https://osf.io/download/692db7dd37baa6ac72b550fb/)). Polymarket's repricing after the debate reversed while its repricing after the assassination attempt persisted ([Tsang & Yang, arXiv 2603.03152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03152)). Prices herded on uninformative timing after the conclave's white smoke ([Sethi](https://rajivsethi.substack.com/p/prediction-markets-and-the-pope)).

## The 2024 election produced one famous call and a contested record

Polymarket's 2024 reputation centers on "Théo," the French trader behind Fredi9999 and related accounts. Polymarket's October 2024 review found that four accounts had spent more than $45M on Trump and found no evidence of manipulation ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/323007/polymarket-manipulation-us-election-liquidity)). Chainalysis later linked **nine accounts** and estimated his net profit at **$78.7M**. Théo told the WSJ he had commissioned private "neighbor" polls, which he never released ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/324996/french-polymarket-whale-us-election-profit-france-ban)). Other outlets put the stake and the profit lower, at $30M+ and about $48–49M ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/11/02/french-whale-polymarket-30-million-donald-trump-election-bet-kamala-harris); [Quartz](https://qz.com/donald-trump-election-betting-polymarket-whale-trader-1851691650)). Any research use should cite the Chainalysis on-chain attribution and acknowledge the spread of estimates.

The most rigorous on-chain treatment is Tsang and Yang's analysis of **2,348,771 matched transactions** in the Trump market. Naive OrderFilled volume overstates exchange-equivalent turnover about **2.5×**: October was **$391M of real turnover against $958M naive**. Moving the October forecast by 5 points cost **$9.1M**. The whale bought **$26.6M of Trump YES, 15.1% of the $176.4M October net inflow**, and capital entered both sides, which the authors read as "heterogeneous beliefs rather than one-sided manipulation" ([arXiv 2603.03136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03136)). Their own arithmetic shows the whale's flow was large enough to move the price several points. The manipulation-versus-information question is therefore partly semantic, and nobody has estimated his causal price impact with a credible identification strategy. In cross-venue terms, Aktuğ and Torul find that Polymarket and Betfair carried about **85% of Hasbrouck information share** across nine venues. Polymarket alone had a midpoint information share of 47.5%, the average cross-venue spread was 6.2 points, and the prediction-market consensus **led S&P 500 futures on election night** ([working paper](https://web.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/pridis.pdf)). Saiegh offers the strongest dissent. Polymarket's final Trump price of 62.65% was the most volatile series in his sample, and he argues markets "were prone to overestimation and excessive volatility" while S&P options did better ([UCSD PDF](https://pages.ucsd.edu/~ssaiegh/forecast_2024.pdf)). His benchmark, electoral-vote share, is not a probability, so his ranking deserves caution.

Clinton and Huang found that buying the cheapest Harris and Trump contracts across Kalshi, PredictIt and Polymarket cost less than $1 on **62 of 65 days**. Price levels correlated around 0.9 across venues, but daily *changes* were nearly uncorrelated ([PDF](https://osf.io/download/692db7dd37baa6ac72b550fb/)). Wash trading also inflated the volume figures that were cited as proof of liquidity: Chaos Labs estimated that about one-third of presidential-market volume was wash trading, and Inca Digital put true volume at about $1.75B against $2.7B reported ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/crypto/2024/10/30/polymarket-trump-election-crypto-wash-trading-researchers/)). The 2024 election is the most studied event in Polymarket's history. Three questions remain open: whether the neighbor-poll signal contained real information, the whale's causal price impact, and proper state-by-state scoring of all seven swing states against polls.

## Integrity research found real money in wash trades, insiders and oracles

Wash-trading estimates span an order of magnitude because the studies measure different things. Sirolly, Ma, Kanoria and Sethi's network method scores wallets by repeated open-and-close behavior and propagates the score through counterparties, exploiting the fact that wash traders cluster with each other while market makers trade with everyone. It flags **about 25% of three years of volume and 14% of 1.26 million wallets**. Wash share peaked near **60% (December 2024 in most coverage)** and ran to about 45% in sports and 17% in elections ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/11/07/polymarket-wash-trading-inflated-prediction-markets-columbia-research); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2025/11/07/polymarket-s-trading-volume-may-be-25-fake-columbia-study-finds); [Sethi Substack](https://rajivsethi.substack.com/p/the-detection-of-wash-trading)). Many flagged clusters made no profit, which points to airdrop or leaderboard farming rather than price manipulation. Dubach's direct same-wallet self-counterparty measure for 2026 finds a **median of just 1% and an upper tail of 22%** ([arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366)). The two figures differ in window, definition and weighting, so they do not contradict each other. A third, mechanical issue compounds both. Paradigm showed that summing OrderFilled events double-counts maker and taker legs ([Paradigm](https://www.paradigm.xyz/2025/12/polymarket-volume-is-being-double-counted)). Mint and burn activity distorts volume further ([Tsang & Yang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.03136)).

In 2026, informed trading became the dominant integrity theme. Mitts and Ofir screen **about 93,000 markets and 50,000 wallets** (Feb 2024–Feb 2026) on five signals: bet size relative to other traders, bet size relative to the trader's own history, profitability, pre-event timing and directional concentration. They flag **210,718 wallet-market pairs with a 69.9% win rate**, more than 60 standard deviations above a permutation null, and estimate **about $143M of anomalous profit** as a lower bound ([Harvard Law Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2026/03/25/from-iran-to-taylor-swift-informed-trading-in-prediction-markets/)). Gómez-Cram, Guo, Kung and Jensen reach a more muted conclusion. They identify **1,950 single-event "insider-like" accounts** that move prices 7–12× more per dollar than skilled traders, but which are too sporadic to drive aggregate accuracy ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study); [Yale Insights](https://insights.som.yale.edu/insights/wisdom-of-the-few-prediction-markets-are-driven-by-small-number-of-skilled-traders)). Market-level detection has hit a wall. Nechepurenko's leakage score is computable for only **88 of 12,708 markets (0.7%)**, and he concludes that resolution semantics, not computation, is the binding constraint ([arXiv 2605.00459](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00459)). The case record has grown fast. It includes the Nobel Peace Prize leak probe ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/polymarket-nobel-probe)) and the SDNY and CFTC complaint against a Google engineer over about $1.2M of "Year in Search" profit ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/402806/google-engineer-insider-trading-polymarket)). It also includes the first federal event-contract insider indictment, unsealed 23 April 2026: an Army Special Forces sergeant allegedly made about **$409,881 on the Maduro operation**, and the parallel CFTC complaint was the agency's first use of the "Eddie Murphy Rule" ([Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2026/04/polymarket-insider-trading-charges-illustrate-doj)). Bubblemaps flagged fresh wallets that earned roughly $1M before the 28 February 2026 Iran strikes ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/391650/fresh-accounts-netted-1-million-on-polymarket-hours-before-us-airstrikes-on-iran-bubblemaps)). Bloomberg flagged about $200M of possible insider trades in the first half of 2026 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/graphics/2026-polymarket-traders-who-knew-the-future/)). Polymarket partnered with Chainalysis on surveillance on 30 April 2026 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-04-30-polymarket-taps-chainalysis-to-police-insider-trading-seeks-15-billion-valuation-399619)), and in August 2026 it reportedly referred dozens of military-linked accounts to the DOJ ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/08/21/politics/polymarket-doj-military-prediction-market)).

The best-documented price manipulation targets the settlement source rather than the traded price. A Stanford/SMU study of about 16,000 five-minute Bitcoin contracts flags **821 wallets (0.3% of 243,000 traders)** whose pattern fits last-second Binance pushes against the Chainlink settlement price. Extending contracts to 15 minutes largely removed the effect ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/crypto/stanford-study-says-polymarket-crypto-bets-are-being-manipulated); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/stanford-study-5-minute-bitcoin-prediction-markets-enable-settlement-manipulation)). Ibrahim and Zaki measure the price of narrative influence. Across 44,976 dislocations of at least 5 points, a market's press-citation rate rises about 33% afterward. Moving a market 5 points *and* getting the move cited costs roughly **$0.7–1.0M**, which they call "epistemic leverage" ([arXiv 2609.06005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06005)).

UMA oracle governance is the least-studied integrity surface, relative to its stakes. In March 2025 a $7M Ukraine minerals-deal market resolved "Yes" without a deal after a whale reportedly cast about 5M UMA tokens, roughly 25% of the vote ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/348171/polymarket-says-governance-attack-by-uma-whale-to-hijack-a-bets-resolution-is-unprecedented)). In July 2025 a **$237M** market on whether Zelenskyy wore a suit was overturned to "No" amid claims that **95% of UMA is whale-held** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/329210/polymarket-rules-no-237m-bet-zelenskyy-suit?amp=1)). UMA's MOOV2 (UMIP-189, August 2025) restricts *proposers* to a whitelist ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/366507/polymarket-uma-oracle-update)). It does not appear to change dispute *voting*. No arXiv or peer-reviewed paper quantifies UMA voter concentration, dispute rates or the resolution-risk premium in prices. The SoK by Rahman, Al-Chami and Clark lists formal oracle guarantees and machine-checkable resolution specifications among its 11 open gaps ([arXiv 2510.15612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15612)).

The regulatory history is well chronicled but thinly analyzed. Polymarket settled with the CFTC for $1.4M in 2022 and geoblocked the US ([CRS IF13187](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13187.html)). The FBI seized the CEO's devices in November 2024 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/326404/fbi-seizes-polymarket-ceos-phone-and-electronics-report)), and DOJ and CFTC closed their probes in July 2025 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/362703/us-justice-department-and-cftc-close-probes-into-polymarket-bloomberg)). Polymarket bought the QCEX exchange for $112M ([Axios](https://axios.com/2025/07/21/prediction-market-polymarket-us)) and received designation as a CFTC-regulated exchange (DCM) in November 2025 ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-03-20_IF13187_33a896165ea2f60e2a49cd3b548cf38ce56a169b.html)). In 2026 it is fighting state preemption battles such as *QCX LLC v. Campbell* ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bloomberg-government-news/polymarket-sues-mssachusetts-to-preempt-state-shutdown)), and a CFTC proposal would replace Rule 40.11's category bans with contract-by-contract review ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13187.html)). The scholarship consists of Mitts and Ofir, law-firm memos, and a qualitative "prediction laundering" critique built on 27 interviews ([arXiv 2602.05181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.05181)). No Polymarket-focused law-review article surfaced.

## Profits flow to makers, bots and a 3% minority

The trader-level literature is the most convergent in this landscape. Akey, Grégoire, Harvie and Martineau study **588 million trades and about $67B**. They find that the **top 1% of users capture 76.5% of profits**, that winners provide liquidity with limit orders, and that longshot buying drives losses ([HEC](https://www.hec.edu/en/faculty-research/events/who-wins-and-who-loses-prediction-markets-evidence-polymarket); [CEPR DP21615](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:cpr:ceprdp:21615)). Gómez-Cram et al. apply a sign-randomization test to 1.72 million accounts and find that **3.14% are skilled**. **44% of those stay skilled out of sample, against about 10% for active mutual funds**, and their order flow predicts both short-term price moves and outcomes. Their conclusion is that accuracy is "the wisdom of an informed minority, not the wisdom of the crowd" ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/398902/skilled-polymarket-traders-are-a-3-minority-and-everyone-else-funds-their-gains-study)). Della Vedova's result is the most striking. Across 222 million resolved trades, retail picks the right side **51.3%** of the time yet loses about **$79M**, while automated traders at **49.9%** accuracy earn about **$133M**. About 70% of the bot edge comes from timing ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/who-profits-from-prediction-markets/); [Marginal Revolution](https://marginalrevolution.com/marginalrevolution/2026/03/who-profits-from-prediction-markets.html)). On Kalshi, Becker finds takers earn −1.12% and makers +1.12% ([jbecker.dev](https://jbecker.dev/research/prediction-market-microstructure)), which suggests the maker-taker transfer is a property of prediction markets in general rather than of Polymarket. Aggregator claims that 70–84% of wallets are unprofitable ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/84-of-polymarket-traders-are-losing-money-0-033-capture-majority-profits)) are very sensitive to definitions and should not be cited over the academic estimates.

The microstructure work carries the most important methodological warning in the field. Dubach archived about **30 billion WebSocket order-book events** over 52 days and joined them to on-chain fills. Trade direction inferred from the feed matches on-chain direction in only **about 59% of buckets**, and the effective half-spread flips sign in 50–67% of markets ([arXiv 2604.24366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24366)). Qin and Yang find the **tick rule at 49.83% and bulk volume classification at 50.51%**, which is effectively a coin flip ([arXiv 2606.04217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04217)). Any spread, Kyle's λ, VPIN or order-flow-imbalance result built on heuristic signing is suspect, and that includes parts of the 2024 election literature. On fees, the only quantitative source is a practitioner analysis. It dates the taker-fee rollout to 5 January 2026 for 15-minute crypto markets, 18 February for sports, and 30 March for most remaining categories, with geopolitics left fee-free. It estimates about $573K per day of net revenue and finds no measurable volume damage, but it does not measure spreads ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/polymarket-fee-rollout)). No academic paper studies the fee rollout, the liquidity-rewards program, or the 2020–2022 AMM era.

Arbitrage and price discovery are reasonably well mapped up to 2025. Saguillo, Ghafouri, Kiffer and Suarez-Tangil (AFT 2025) estimate **$39.6M of realized arbitrage** from April 2024 to April 2025. About **$29M** came from NegRisk multi-outcome rebalancing, about **$10.6M** from single-market YES+NO trades, and only about **$95K** from cross-market logical dependencies. The top account earned $2.0M ([arXiv 2508.03474](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.03474)). Gebele, Mutzel and Matthes show that the protocol constrains this: payoff-bound violations on the YES side persist because they cannot be executed, while executable NO-side violations are rarer. Their estimate of executable NegRisk profit is about **$1.12M** ([arXiv 2608.00666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00666)). By 2026, in live NBA markets, Cheng, Yang and Zou found only **7 single-market episodes (median 3.6 seconds)** across 75 million snapshots, and combinatorial opportunities averaged just **14.8 executable shares** ([arXiv 2605.00864](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00864)). YES+NO parity deviations in the 2024 election market shrank from a half-life of about two hours in April to **under one minute by October** ([Tsang & Yang](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.03136v2)). Across venues, Ng, Peng, Tao and Zhou find Polymarket led Kalshi around the 2024 election. Across matched 2023–2026 markets, Kalshi leads in sports and Polymarket in politics and entertainment ([SSRN 5331995](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=5331995); full text not accessed). Three things are missing: a fee-adjusted, timestamp-synchronized Polymarket–Kalshi arbitrage study, any arbitrage study after the fee rollout, and logical-dependency arbitrage beyond election pairs.

## LLMs still lose to the Polymarket price at matched timestamps

Polymarket has become a standard source of resolved questions and of a market baseline for AI forecasting. The consistent result is that frontier systems approach crowd level but do not beat the contemporaneous price. Halawi et al. built the first retrieval-augmented forecaster and drew 599 of their questions from Polymarket. The system scored a **Brier of 0.179 against the crowd's 0.149**, and a system-plus-crowd ensemble did best at **0.146** ([arXiv 2402.18563](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18563v1)). Lightning Rod's Foresight-32B scored **0.199 on 251 live Polymarket questions, against 0.170 for the market itself** ([HF model card](https://huggingface.co/LightningRodLabs/foresight-32B/blob/main/README.md)). The sharpest evidence is an August 2026 audit of ForecastBench market questions. Simply copying the price shown to forecasters scores **70.93** on the Brier Index. Of 527 entries tested, **none significantly beat that copier line and 442 were significantly worse**, while superforecasters sit 4.67 points above it ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8YuG9EuhyYqaNBt7F/the-copier-line-what-forecastbench-s-market-scores-actually)). The Forecasting Research Institute's July 2026 claim of AI-superforecaster parity ([FRI](https://forecastingresearch.substack.com/p/ai-models-have-likely-reached-parity)) is strongest on the auto-generated dataset questions and contested on market questions ([Good Judgment](https://goodjudgment.substack.com/p/what-forecastbench-doesnt-measure)).

The 2026 Polymarket benchmarks have shifted attention to leakage control. Hindcast pairs 7,913 markets with a timestamp-locked Reddit archive of 18.3 million documents; retrieval cut Qwen3-32B's Brier by 23% but hurt on hype-prone topics ([arXiv 2607.14051](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.14051)). LEAF reports that pre-cutoff evaluation inflates performance by about 147% on equity tasks ([arXiv 2605.16358](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.16358v2)). RMF samples 3,000 questions from **more than 600,000 resolved Polymarket markets** but omits any comparison with the market price, which the authors list as a limitation ([arXiv 2609.32885](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.32885)). Trading agents fare worse than forecasters. In PolyBench, only **2 of 7 models were profitable** after sweeping historical books ([arXiv 2604.14199](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14199v1)). In Prediction Arena's live trading, Polymarket agents averaged **−1.1%** and all six Kalshi agents lost money ([arXiv 2604.07355](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.07355v1)). On the training side, outcome-based reinforcement learning on about 10,000 resolved Polymarket questions matched o1 with better calibration and showed **more than 10% ROI in a trading simulation** ([Turtel et al., arXiv 2505.17989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17989)). Note that two widely cited benchmarks, Prophet Arena and KalshiBench, use Kalshi, not Polymarket ([arXiv 2510.17638](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17638v2)). The open questions are concrete. No paper trains on market probabilities as soft labels. No study puts the Polymarket price, superforecasters and frontier LLMs on identical questions at identical timestamps. No audited record exists of a live LLM agent trading on Polymarket for more than three months.

## Applied users mostly borrowed Polymarket for one election

In finance, the mature use is high-frequency identification of election effects. Aktuğ and Torul combine 5-minute Polymarket probabilities with OpenSecrets data on 71 firms. A **1-point rise in Trump's win probability opens a 0.25% return gap** between Republican- and Democrat-aligned firms, and the gap persists for about 60 trading days. They also document a three-tier lead-lag hierarchy: Polymarket and Betfair lead, sharp sportsbooks follow within hours, and Unibet lags by at least 12 hours ([PDF](https://web.bogazici.edu.tr/torul/polcap.pdf)). A Finance Research Letters paper finds Trump Media stock took 3–5 days to absorb shocks to Polymarket election odds ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612325001448)). Macro work, by contrast, has gone to Kalshi. The Fed staff paper by Diercks, Katz and Wright ([FEDS 2026-010](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2026010pap.pdf)) and Angelini's CPI belief distributions ([arXiv 2606.30040](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30040)) are both Kalshi-only. Eichengreen and coauthors use Polymarket on Fed independence, but their findings were not retrieved ([Eichengreen research page](https://eml.berkeley.edu/~eichengr/research.shtml)). **No study uses Polymarket's tariff, recession, shutdown or debt-ceiling odds** as policy-uncertainty shocks, even though press snapshots show Polymarket and futures diverging sharply ahead of FOMC meetings ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/polymarket-prices-53-odds-of-fed-rate-hike-in-september-2026-vs-32-in-futures)).

Political science has moved from asking whether markets beat polls to asking what happens when odds become media. Polymarket odds now run through X/xAI and, since January 2026, Dow Jones titles including the WSJ and Barron's ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/384646/polymarket-to-provide-prediction-data-to-wall-street-journal-barrons-in-dow-jones-deal)). Nechepurenko theorizes prices as self-fulfilling focal points ([arXiv 2604.24147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24147)), and Ibrahim and Zaki measure press citation of odds. Yet "there is as yet no empirical basis" for how published market odds affect voters ([Israel Democracy Institute](https://en.idi.org.il/articles/66410)). The nearest experimental precedent covers 538-style probabilities and predates Polymarket ([Westwood, Messing & Lelkes](https://www.asc.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2021-03/Projecting%20Confidence-%20How%20the%20Probabilistic%20Horse%20Race%20Confuses%20and%20Demobilizes%20the%20Public.pdf)). The only wallet-level partisanship work is a behavioral "political leaning score" study from 2024 ([arXiv 2407.14844](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.14844)).

Sports is Polymarket's largest category, at **39% of volume since July 2024** ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/05/27/trading-volume-on-prediction-markets-has-soared-in-recent-months/)), and among its least studied. Beyond the NBA arbitrage paper and the finding that the FLB is absent in sports, the evidence consists of vendor blogs with unverified numbers ([Polymarket Analytics](https://polymarketanalytics.com/research/nba-sportsbooks-vs-prediction-markets); [OddsPapi](https://oddspapi.io/blog/prediction-market-accuracy-backtest-polymarket-sportsbooks/)). There is no study against Pinnacle or Betfair closing lines, no in-play study, and nothing on the 2026 World Cup.

In crypto, Portnaya finds a persistent **5.6–6.3 point gap** between Polymarket BTC threshold prices and Binance option-implied binaries, with an AR(1) half-life of about four hours. The gap widens to about 11 points against Deribit and is largest in the tails ([arXiv 2606.19517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19517)). A hobbyist study finds Polymarket daily brackets beat DVOL-implied probabilities at intraday horizons ([GitHub implied-odds](https://github.com/ivanalonso-dev/implied-odds)). The OpenMarket Polymarket–Binance dataset now enables proper lead-lag work ([arXiv 2607.26245](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26245v1)). The 5- and 15-minute up/down markets, reportedly bot-dominated ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/trending/prediction-markets-are-turning-into-a-bot-playground/)), have no peer-reviewed microstructure study.

Geopolitical markets are studied almost entirely as an integrity problem, not as a measurement tool. Nobody has scored ceasefire or strike-timing markets against Metaculus or Good Judgment, or built a geopolitical-risk index from them. The one disease-surveillance paper uses **13 Kalshi** markets ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44482-026-00042-6)). Polymarket's roughly $3M hantavirus market, which resolves on WHO "pandemic" wording, has drawn only press coverage ([Mother Jones](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2026/05/polymarket-prediction-market-hantavirus-pandemic-epidemic-betting/)).

## The 2026 data stack broke old pipelines

Most pre-2026 tutorials, notebooks and bots are now wrong in at least one important respect, so a new project should build from the current documentation. On **28 April 2026**, Polymarket cut over to **CLOB V2**. The cutover brought new exchange contracts, an EIP-712 domain version 2, and a new order struct with no nonce or feeRateBps, and fees are now set at match time. **pUSD**, a USDC-backed collateral token, replaced USDC.e, all open V1 orders were wiped, and the legacy SDKs "no longer work against production" ([Polymarket V2 migration](https://docs.polymarket.com/v2-migration)). `py-clob-client` was archived on 25 May 2026 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Polymarket/py-clob-client)). The official pages name the successor SDK inconsistently (`py-clob-client-v2`, `py-sdk`, `polymarket-client`). The **Data API v1 retires on 24 October 2026**. Version 2 replaces v1's 10,000-row offset cap with cursor pagination, switches fields to snake_case and renames `market` to `condition` ([Data API migration](https://docs.polymarket.com/migrate/data-api-v1-to-v2.md)). Goldsky now warns that the legacy public subgraphs "will return incomplete or incorrect data" after V2 and points users to paid Turbo Pipeline datasets ([Goldsky](https://docs.goldsky.com/chains/polymarket)). Polymarket's acquisition of Dome on 19 February 2026 ended Dome's unified Polymarket–Kalshi API on 28 April ([The Block](https://theblock.co/post/390546/polymarket-buys-fresh-prediction-market-api-startup-dome-marking-second-official-acquisition); [Dome docs](https://docs.domeapi.io/index.md)).

Four practical consequences follow. First, any project touching the **2026 midterms must start live WebSocket or 1-minute recording before Election Day**, because fine-grained price history decays from the official API within days to weeks and no free historical L2 book exists. Second, every time series crossing 28 April 2026 must stitch the V1 and V2 exchange addresses, or it will break at the migration. Third, every volume figure must declare its definition: one- or two-sided, MINT/MERGE in or out, and wash-filtered or not. Fourth, trade direction must come from on-chain settlement rather than feed or tick-rule inference.

Copy-trading and leaderboard-selection effects, partisan wallet behavior, and fee-rebate economics for makers are also unstudied. These are smaller bets that could fold into ranks 1, 4 or 7. The top four directions share an advantage over the rest: their identifying variation comes from discrete 2026 events that no published paper has yet exploited. Each also has a clear pre- and post-period available in data a solo researcher can afford.

## Conclusion

The Polymarket literature has moved quickly from "does it predict?" to "who makes it predict, and at whose expense?". The emerging answer reframes the platform. Its accuracy is real but conditional. A small, persistent, partly automated and occasionally privileged minority produces that accuracy, a losing majority pays for it, and the mechanics of the protocol, not just traders' beliefs, constrain efficiency. The next contribution should therefore treat Polymarket less as a black-box probability feed and more as an institution whose design choices are now shifting under researchers' feet. Fees, collateral, settlement oracles, surveillance and media distribution all changed in 2026, and each change is a quasi-experiment waiting for a paper.

The window for some of this work is short. The midterms fall in three weeks, fine-grained history disappears within days, and the Data API v1 sunsets on 24 October. A project that starts by capturing high-frequency data and building a V2-aware, wash-filtered, on-chain-signed dataset will be positioned for the highest-value questions above. Beyond that, the most defensible research strategy is to pair Polymarket with an external benchmark the field has ignored: Pinnacle closing lines, Deribit densities, UMA vote records, or Kalshi's Fed-endorsed macro ladders. Polymarket-only studies of calibration and profitability have reached diminishing returns, while studies that compare it with an outside benchmark have barely begun.
